How this works

Opportunity score is a composite signal. It is there to help compare genera on one commercial scale, not to replace the ingredient metrics.

  1. We combine the main commercial ingredients, including scarcity, demand, and how quickly stock tends to return.
  2. We calculate the result for the focus genus and the active benchmark, whether that is each peer, a peer average, or the market baseline.
  3. We use the score as a comparison aid, then keep the ingredient table below visible so you can still see why one genus is ahead.

A higher score means demand looks stronger relative to supply. A small gap means the comparison is competitive rather than decisive.

How this works

Stability score is not a demand or stock metric on its own. It is a confidence-style signal about whether the genus keeps looking commercially strong over time.

  1. We calculate the genus's commercial result over several recent windows.
  2. We check whether its rank and score stay fairly consistent from one window to the next.
  3. We reduce the score when the result swings around a lot or when there is not enough history.

High stability means the result looks repeatable. Lower stability means the commercial case may depend too much on one recent period or limited history.

How this works

Defensible preference is a judgment layer. It turns the raw comparison into a plain-English answer to a practical question: does your preferred genus still hold up commercially, or are you forcing the data to flatter it?

  1. We compare the focus genus with the current benchmark on the same opportunity scale.
  2. We look at how far behind it is, or whether it stays close enough to remain commercially credible.
  3. We translate that gap into a judgment such as yes, borderline, or no.

A positive read means preference and market logic are still fairly aligned. A negative read means the commercial case is now meaningfully weaker than the leading alternative.
